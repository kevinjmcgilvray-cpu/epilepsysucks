The road ahead

Smart over stiff

As of October 10, 2026, I'm in taper week heading into my next race — the SLO Half Marathon (13.1 miles) on Sunday, October 18. Today's run was an 8-mile taper, just keeping the legs fresh.

My current routine: running 5 days a week on top of roughly 10 miles of walking at my job, 5 days a week — about 50 miles of walking before I even lace up. Friday and Sunday are off from running on purpose, so running-specific endurance can build without overloading legs that are already walking a half-marathon's worth every single week. Weekdays stay easy — 2 to 4 comfortable miles, Monday through Thursday — and Saturday is the one day that actually builds anything.

Training with epilepsy, three brain surgeries, and a DBS implant means my body gets the final say over any number on a schedule. A few rules I don't break:

  • The Kevin Rule. The schedule is a guide, not a debt. If work's exhausted my legs, I shorten the run. If recovery's poor, I rest. If I have a seizure, I recover first and follow the return-to-exercise plan I built with my medical team — not the training plan.
  • The golden rule. Never make up missed mileage. Never cram two long runs together. The goal is arriving in Los Angeles healthy enough to finish all 26.2 — not a perfect log.

March 7, 2027 · ASICS Los Angeles Marathon

Stadium to the Stars

From Dodger Stadium to Avenue of the Stars in Century City — a USATF-certified Boston Qualifier, point-to-point through Chinatown, Hollywood, Beverly Hills, and Brentwood. On paper it’s a net downhill. In practice, it’s a rolling-hill race that punishes anyone who believes the brochure.

How the course actually runs

Miles 1–3 dump you out of Dodger Stadium on a steep downhill — the trap that burns people who go out too hot. Miles 4–6 answer with short, sharp rollers through Echo Park and Silver Lake. Miles 10–18 roll through Hollywood and West Hollywood: mostly flat with a short climb around mile 12, then a cruise toward Beverly Hills.

The fight is late. Miles 20–24 — the San Vicente out-and-back — drop hard (nearly −5% in places), then climb back. That’s the quad-buster where most PR attempts are won or lost. Hold something for Brentwood, or Century City will feel like a different race.
